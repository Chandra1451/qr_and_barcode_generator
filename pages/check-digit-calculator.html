What the check digit is for

The last digit of an EAN, UPC or other GTIN is a check digit. It is calculated from all the other digits, so a scanner or a system can tell when a number has been misread or mistyped. GS1 US puts it this way: the check digit "helps to confirm the integrity of your barcode number" (GS1 US check digit calculator).

How the check digit is worked out

GS1 uses one method for every fixed-length numeric number it defines, including GTIN-8, GTIN-12, GTIN-13 and GTIN-14 (GS1 General Specifications, section 7.9.1):

  1. Starting with the digit next to where the check digit goes, multiply the digits by 3, 1, 3, 1 and so on, moving left.
  2. Add up the results.
  3. Subtract the total from the nearest equal or higher multiple of ten. The answer is the check digit (0 if the total is already a multiple of ten).

Worked example. 952012345678 uses the 952 prefix, which GS1 keeps for examples, so it isn't anyone's product.

The results add up to 102. The nearest multiple of ten at or above it is 110, and 110 − 102 = 8. So the check digit is 8 and the full GTIN-13 is 9520123456788. You can try it in the calculator above.

Which numbers this works for

  • GTIN-13: EAN-13 barcodes, and ISBN-13 book numbers, which use the same check digit.
  • GTIN-12: UPC-A barcodes.
  • GTIN-14: numbers for cases and cartons, usually printed as ITF-14.

Adding zeros in front of a number doesn't change its check digit, because the method works from the right. A UPC-A with a 0 in front gives the same check digit as a 13-digit number.

Not covered here: GTIN-8 and SSCC numbers use the same method but have no matching barcode in our studio yet, and old 10-digit ISBNs use a different calculation.

A correct check digit does not make a number yours

Any string of digits can be given a correct check digit, so a correct check digit only shows that the number is well formed. It does not mean the number was issued to you, or to anyone.

That is why this page has no "generate a GTIN" button. Barcode numbers for products sold in shops and on marketplaces come from GS1. Our guides explain how to get them: how to get a barcode for your product and how to get a barcode for products in India.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this calculator give me a barcode number for my product? ▼

No. It only works out or checks the last digit of a number you already have. Barcode numbers for products sold in shops come from GS1.

Why does my number fail the check? ▼

Usually a digit is wrong, missing or in the wrong order, or the wrong format is selected. A 12-digit UPC-A entered with GTIN-13 selected is treated as 12 digits to calculate from, not a number to check: choose GTIN-12, or add a 0 in front.

What is the check digit of a UPC-A? ▼

It is the 12th digit. Enter the first 11 digits with GTIN-12 selected to work it out.

Are the numbers I type sent anywhere? ▼

No. The calculation runs in your browser, and the numbers you type or paste are not sent to us.

Does it work for 10-digit ISBNs? ▼

No. ISBN-10 uses a different calculation. ISBN-13 numbers, which start with 978 or 979, work with GTIN-13.
