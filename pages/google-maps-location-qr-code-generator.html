How to make, print and test a Maps QR code

Step by step

  1. Type your place name or address, for example "Blue Door Café, 12 High Street, Leeds". Search for it in Google Maps first and copy the wording that finds the right result.
  2. For a spot with no address (an event entrance, a car park, a trailhead), fill in latitude and longitude instead. In Google Maps, long-press the exact spot (right-click on a computer) and copy the numbers shown.
  3. Coordinates are used if both are filled in; otherwise the place name or address is searched.
  4. Scan the preview with your phone and check that Google Maps opens the right place.
  5. Download SVG or PNG for flyers, signs and business cards.

Printing & size

  • Size: at least 2 cm (0.8 in) wide for flyers and cards read up close; for a door or window sign read from 1–2 metres away, about 10–20 cm (4–8 in). This follows a common rule of thumb, not part of the QR standard: make the code at least a tenth of the scanning distance. Test-scan at the real distance before printing.
  • Add a line of text such as "Scan for directions" and the address, so people know what the code does and can still find you without scanning.
  • Keep the white border around the code, and print dark on light.

Common mistakes

  • A vague place name that matches several places ("Main Street Café"). Add the street and town, or use coordinates.
  • Coordinates in the wrong order. Latitude comes first (north–south, −90 to 90), then longitude (east–west, −180 to 180).
  • Pinning the middle of a big site instead of the visitor entrance.
  • Not test-scanning on both iPhone and Android before printing.

Why use Maps QR

Opens straight in Google Maps

One scan opens the place in the Google Maps app (or the browser), where visitors tap Directions to get there.

Works without an address

Coordinates can pin a car park, a trailhead or an event gate that has no street address.

No address to retype

Visitors don't type anything, so there is less room for typos or a similarly named street in another town.

Is a Maps QR code the right fit?

A shop, restaurant or office with a normal address?

Type the business name and town. Then scan the preview and check it opens your place, not one with a similar name.

No address, or an entrance that is hard to find?

Use coordinates for the exact gate or door rather than the middle of the site.

Want people to see your reviews and opening hours?

Copy the share link of your Google Maps listing and turn it into a Website URL QR code instead. See the questions below.

Rules to know

Before you print a batch

  • Scan the printed code on an iPhone and an Android phone, and check it opens the right place.
  • Point coordinates at the public entrance, not a private loading dock or the middle of a large building.

Common questions

Can I use latitude and longitude instead of an address? ▼

Yes. Fill in both boxes with decimal numbers, latitude first (for example 37.7749 and -122.4194). This is the best option for places with no street address, such as a field used for a festival or the side gate of a venue.

Does the code start turn-by-turn navigation? ▼

Not straight away. It opens Google Maps with a pin on the place. From there the person taps Directions, and Google Maps routes them from wherever they are.

How do I find the coordinates of a spot? ▼

On a computer, right-click the spot in Google Maps; the coordinates are the first line of the menu, and clicking them copies them. On a phone, touch and hold the spot until a pin drops, and the coordinates appear at the top of the screen or in the place details.

Will the code expire? ▼

The code itself does not expire. The Maps link is stored inside it, with no redirect through us and no subscription to lapse. It keeps working for as long as Google supports this link format, which it documents for public use.

Does the QR code open Google Maps or Apple Maps? ▼

Google Maps. If the Google Maps app is installed it opens there; otherwise the place opens in the phone's web browser on Google Maps. It does not open Apple Maps or Waze.

I already have a Google Maps share link. Can I use that? ▼

Yes. In Google Maps, tap Share and copy the link, then make a Website link QR code with it. That opens the exact place you shared, including its name and reviews.

Will the QR code still work if my business moves? ▼

It keeps pointing to the old place, because the location is stored in the code itself. Make and print a new code after a move.
