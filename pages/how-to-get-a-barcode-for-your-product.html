How to Get a Barcode for Your Product

For a retail product, first obtain or confirm the product identifier your retailer requires. Then make the barcode image that carries it. This guide explains the difference between a GTIN and its barcode, how to choose the matching generator, and what to check before printing.

The short answer

  1. Check the receiving retailer or marketplace's requirements. Confirm the identifier type, who assigns it and whether an exception applies.
  2. Obtain or confirm the assigned number. For GS1 identifiers, use the organisation serving your country and check its current licensing options.
  3. Match the number to its carrier. Use EAN-13 for a GTIN-13 and UPC-A for a GTIN-12. Check how your recipient expects any padded database representation to be supplied.
  4. Make the image, then check the file and printed proof. Compare the decoded number with the source record before printing in volume.

For stock-room bins or company assets, see internal identifiers.

GTIN, EAN-13 and UPC-A: what's the difference?

GTIN (Global Trade Item Number) is the product number. EAN-13 and UPC-A are the barcodes that carry it at the checkout.

NumberDigitsBarcodeWhere it's used
GTIN-1313EAN-13Retail almost everywhere outside the US and Canada
GTIN-1212UPC-ARetail in the US and Canada
GTIN-1414ITF-14Outer cartons and cases, not consumer packs
ISBN-1313EAN-13 (Bookland)Books, from your national ISBN agency, not GS1

Put a 0 in front of a 12-digit UPC-A number and you have the same product's 13-digit number. Checkout scanners around the world read both barcodes, so a UPC-A product can be sold in Europe and an EAN-13 product in the US. Some US retailers still ask suppliers for UPC-A, so check what your buyer wants. More: UPC vs EAN-13: which barcode do you need?

Step 1: Decide whether you need a GS1 number

Start with the organisation that will receive or scan the code. Retailers and marketplaces may require GS1 identifiers, but the rules depend on the recipient, category and market. Check before buying numbers or printing packaging. For an existing product, confirm its assigned identifier before obtaining another one.

Selling on Amazon involves two jobs. The listing uses a product identifier such as a GTIN, unless an applicable exemption is approved. For FBA stock, check which barcode must be on each unit: an accepted manufacturer barcode or an Amazon barcode (FNSKU), according to the current rules for your marketplace and seller/product status. An FNSKU comes from Seller Central and is separate from the retail GTIN.

See Do I need a barcode to sell on Amazon? for the US/India decision guide, and check current Seller Central instructions before preparing stock. Amazon explains listing identifiers and FBA labelling separately.

For identifiers used only inside your own inventory system, such as stock-room bins or company assets, Code 128 can carry your own SKU or asset number. Choose a format your software and scanners accept. Keep internal identifiers separate from retail GTINs and check requirements when a trading partner receives the labels.

Step 2: Get your numbers from GS1

  • Go to gs1.org and choose your country, or go straight to your national office, for example GS1 US, GS1 UK or GS1 India.
  • Most offices license a company prefix: a block of numbers sized to how many products you plan to sell. GS1 US also sells single GTINs for sellers with only a few products.
  • Fees depend on the country and the number of products. A company prefix usually has a yearly renewal fee; GS1 US single GTINs are a one-time purchase. Check your office's current price list.
Avoid cheap numbers from resellers. Those numbers were licensed to another company, and the GS1 database still shows that company as the owner. Marketplaces and retailers that check the database can reject or remove your listings.

Step 3: Give every product variant its own number

  • Each item sold separately needs its own GTIN: 250 ml and 500 ml are two numbers; red and blue are two numbers; a single bar and a 6-pack are two numbers.
  • Keep a simple spreadsheet: number, product name, size or variant, and the date you assigned it.
  • Do not reuse a GTIN for a different product. Follow the GS1 GTIN Management Standard when assigning numbers or changing products and packs.

Step 4: The check digit (done for you)

The last digit of every EAN-13 and UPC-A is a check digit. It helps detect some entry or reading errors. Our generator calculates it when you type 12 digits (EAN-13) or 11 digits (UPC-A), and checks it if you type the full number. Example only: these digits are not an identifier issued to you. Here is how the calculation works, using 590123412345:

  1. Starting from the rightmost digit, multiply the digits alternately by 3 and 1: 5×3, 4×1, 3×3, 2×1, 1×3, 4×1, 3×3, 2×1, 1×3, 0×1, 9×3, 5×1.
  2. Add the results: 15 + 4 + 9 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 9 + 2 + 3 + 0 + 27 + 5 = 83.
  3. The check digit is what you add to reach the next multiple of 10: 90 − 83 = 7.

See GS1 UK's check-digit explanation. If your product record already includes the last digit, use the complete number and compare the result with that record.

So the full EAN-13 is 5901234123457. The same rule works for UPC-A: 03600029145 gets check digit 2.

Step 5: Make the barcode

  1. Open the EAN-13 generator for a 13-digit number or the UPC-A generator for a 12-digit number.
  2. Type your number. A complete 12-digit GTIN-12 goes in UPC-A; only use the EAN-13 tool's 12-digit input for the unfinished data portion of a GTIN-13. Keep the human-readable digits switched on.
  3. Download SVG for packaging artwork, since it stays sharp at any size and your designer can place it exactly. Use PNG at 4× for documents and labels.
  4. For sticker labels, use the label maker with Avery 5160 and similar sheets, or a thermal roll. To make many at once, use the batch generator.

Step 6: Print consumer-item EAN/UPC at the right size

MeasurementEAN-13UPC-A
Target size (narrowest bar 0.330 mm)37.29 mm (1.468 in) wide including margins, bars 22.85 mm (0.900 in) high
Smallest for retail checkout (bar 0.264 mm, "80%")29.83 mm wide, bars 18.28 mm high
Largest (bar 0.660 mm, "200%")74.58 mm wide, bars 45.70 mm high
Blank margin (quiet zone)11 bar-widths left, 7 right9 bar-widths each side

These consumer-item reference dimensions come from the GS1 Sweden size guide. Extra export padding can increase the full-file dimensions; preserve it and check the actual symbol at its final size.

  • Don't shorten the bars to fit a small pack. Use a smaller size within the allowed range instead.
  • Dark bars on a light background. Black on white is best. Follow GS1 UK's colour guidance when choosing artwork colours.
  • Matte surfaces scan better than glossy ones, and the barcode shouldn't wrap around a tight curve.

Step 7: Test before you print in volume

  1. Compare the decoded number. Open an exported PNG in a compatible reader, such as our scanner, and compare every digit with the assigned number.
  2. Check a printed proof. Use the final size, stock and equipment for the intended workflow where available. Retest after changing size, colour, material or placement.
  3. Check verification requirements. If your retailer requests a barcode-quality report, arrange its verification process in addition to the scan test.

What about 2D barcodes? (GS1 Sunrise 2027)

GS1's 2027 ambition concerns retailer readiness to read both existing and newer barcode carriers. Follow your trading partners' current packaging instructions when adding a 2D code. This site's ordinary QR and Data Matrix generators do not by themselves establish a retail GS1 workflow. See GS1's 2D readiness guidance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just make up my own barcode number? ▼

For internal bins or assets, Code 128 can carry identifiers you define. For retail products, use the assigned product identifier and check the receiving organisation's requirements before making labels.

How much does a product barcode cost? ▼

Identifier fees depend on your GS1 organisation, licensing option and number of products. Some options have renewal fees; GS1 US also offers individual GTINs without annual renewal. Check current official options for your country. UniversalCodeMaker currently charges no fee to generate the image.

Should I use EAN-13 or UPC-A? ▼

Use the barcode that matches your number: EAN-13 for a 13-digit GTIN, UPC-A for a 12-digit GTIN. Use the assigned number rather than choosing by sales country alone. Ask your GS1 office or receiving retailer if you are unsure.

Can I use the same barcode on Amazon and in shops? ▼

Yes. A GTIN identifies the product itself, so the same number works on Amazon, in stores and with distributors. Amazon may also ask for an FNSKU label on FBA stock, which is a separate Amazon-only code.

Do I need a new barcode if I change the packaging? ▼

Usually not for a small design change. A new number is needed when the product changes in a way buyers or stores need to tell apart, such as a different size, quantity or variant. GS1 publishes detailed rules for when a new GTIN is required.
